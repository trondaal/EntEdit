Learning entity-oriented metadata with EntEdit

Activity: user tasks and data quality

In this activity we look at the data from the user's side: what it lets a user do when searching and exploring, and what that tells us about its quality.

The IFLA LRM user tasks

IFLA LRM describes five generic tasks that bibliographic data should support:

  • Find: bring together information about one or more resources of interest by searching on any relevant criteria.
  • Identify: understand clearly what the resources found are, and tell similar resources apart.
  • Select: decide which of the resources found suit the user's needs, and set aside those that do not.
  • Obtain: get access to the content of the resource.
  • Explore: discover resources through their relationships to other resources, and place them in context.

Search and display in EntEdit

EntEdit's search is built on full-text indexes that GraphDB keeps of the entities and their relationships. A search finds results that contain all the words typed; a word ending in * also matches longer words. The search has three views, each with its own index:

  • Publications (manifestations), the default. A publication is found by its own titles and statement of responsibility, and by the names and titles of what it embodies: its expressions, their works, and works they are translations, adaptations or parts of. Each result shows the publication with its content: a single expression is shown as part of the entry, several are listed under Contents.
  • Contents (expressions). Each result shows an expression with its work, its creators and contributors, its language and type of content, and its relationships; its publications can be opened below it.
  • Works. Each result shows a work with its creators and relationships; its expressions can be opened below it, and the publications of each expression below that.

Beside the results, filters with hit counts narrow them by category of work, genre or form, language and content type, and for publications also by media and carrier type. The names and titles in a result are links: following one searches for that person or entity. Relationships to many entities are shown as a count that opens a list. Every result also links to a graph view of the entity and its relationships.

The Edit tab gives a second way in: lists of all entities of a class, filtered by their names.
