Learning entity-oriented metadata with EntEdit

Activity: user tasks and data quality

In this activity we look at the data from the user's side: what it lets a user do when searching and exploring, and what that tells us about its quality.

The IFLA LRM user tasks

IFLA LRM describes five generic tasks that bibliographic data should support:

  • Find: bring together information about one or more resources of interest by searching on any relevant criteria.
  • Identify: understand clearly what the resources found are, and tell similar resources apart.
  • Select: decide which of the resources found suit the user's needs, and set aside those that do not.
  • Obtain: get access to the content of the resource.
  • Explore: discover resources through their relationships to other resources, and place them in context.

Search and display in EntEdit

EntEdit's search is built on full-text indexes that GraphDB keeps of the entities and their relationships. A search finds results that contain all the words typed; a word ending in * also matches longer words. The search has three views, each with its own index:

  • Publications (manifestations), the default. A publication is found by its own titles and statement of responsibility, and by the names and titles of what it embodies: its expressions, their works, and works they are translations, adaptations or parts of. Each result shows the publication with its content: a single expression is shown as part of the entry, several are listed under Contents.
  • Contents (expressions). Each result shows an expression with its work, its creators and contributors, its language and type of content, and its relationships; its publications can be opened below it.
  • Works. Each result shows a work with its creators and relationships; its expressions can be opened below it, and the publications of each expression below that.

Beside the results, filters with hit counts narrow them by category of work, genre or form, language and content type, and for publications also by media and carrier type. The names and titles in a result are links: following one searches for that person or entity. Relationships to many entities are shown as a count that opens a list. Every result also links to a graph view of the entity and its relationships.

The Edit tab gives a second way in: lists of all entities of a class, filtered by their names.

The activity

Work in your group and use the Search tab, either in the database you used in the earlier activities or, for a larger dataset, in the examples repository. The searches suggested below are for the data from the earlier activities; in the examples, choose your own: an author, a work with many expressions, a collection. The aim is not to test the search, but to use it as a way of looking at the data: each choice about how to show the entities is also a question about how they should be described. Take one part at a time, and note what you would show to a user, what you would leave out, and what you would change in the data.

1. The hierarchy in three views

  • Search for Ackroyd in Publications, Contents and Works. Where do the English original, the translations and the audiobook appear, and how is the hierarchy of work, expression and manifestation shown in each view?
  • In Works, open the work, then an expression, then its publications. Is the order of the levels the one a user would expect?
  • Which view would you start from for each of the user tasks above, and which would you make the default?

2. Presenting an entity and its links

  • Look at one result closely. What lets a user identify it and tell it from the others, and what helps them select it? Is anything missing, or repeated?
  • Follow the links: a name, a work title, a related work. What does the search show for each, and is it what a user who clicked would expect? Remove the link to go back.
  • Try the filters. Do the categories and the counts make sense with your data?

3. Which relationships to show

  • Search for Little women. Which relationships are shown, and at which level: work, expression or publication? A relationship can be stated from either entity: does it matter for the display which side your group chose?
  • Some relationships are shown as a count that opens a list. Where does that help, and where would you rather see the targets at once?
  • Hide relationships with the note under the filters (“Change”), or under Settings. Which ones help users explore, and which ones only add noise? Would the answer be the same for a different user, or a larger collection?

4. Collections and aggregates

  • Search for Lord of the rings, Tree and leaf and Perilous realm in Publications. How are the boxed sets and the collections shown, and what do their Contents list?
  • A publication of a single expression is shown as one entry. So is a publication of a single collection, when the collection's work has a genre or form marked as a collection (a short story collection or an anthology, for example); its parts are then listed under Contents. Find an example in your data, or make one, and compare it with a publication whose works simply follow one another.
  • Look at the same titles in Works. Which collections appear as works of their own, and should they? Compare with the book that holds two of Christie's novels.
  • How would you present a boxed set, a collection with a title of its own and a collection without one, so that a user can find both the whole and the parts?

5. Data quality

  • What did searching show about your own data: missing links, a publication with no content, the same work entered twice, a relationship stated at one level in one place and at another elsewhere?
  • Which of these would a user notice, and which user task would fail because of it?

Before the plenary, agree on two or three observations to share: one about how the search presents the data, and one about the data itself.
